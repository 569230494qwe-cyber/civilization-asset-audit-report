# The Bee and the Wolf

## I. First, Watch a Bee

Crouch down. Watch a bee.

It lands on a flower. Furry legs, transparent wings, a straw of a mouth sliding into the heart of the blossom. It drinks the nectar. Finishes one flower, flies to the next. Flies back to the hive, spits the honey out. Flies out again. Hundreds of trips a day. Dozens of kilometers. Dozens of days. Then it dies.

It does not know the word "industrious."
It does not know the phrase "selfless devotion."
It does not know how many poems humans have written about it, how many fairy tales they have spun, how many certificates of merit they have awarded it.

It is simply gathering nectar. It is simply flying. It simply is.

You watch it. You watch for a whole afternoon. And you find that you cannot stuff the word "industrious" into its body. The word is too big. Too big. A bee cannot contain it.

## II. Then Watch a Wolf

Lift your eyes. Watch a wolf.

It crouches in the grass, gray fur flat along its spine. Its eyes hold on what is ahead. Ahead of it, a rabbit. It has waited a long time. The rabbit stirs. It bursts out. Running. Pouncing. Tearing. It has eaten its fill. It licks its mouth. It walks away.

It does not know the word "evil."
It does not know the phrase "law of the jungle."
It does not know how many horror stories humans have cast it in, how many "big bad wolf" fables they have woven, how many curses like "wolf-hearted, dog-lunged" they have hurled.

It is simply hunting. It is simply running. It simply is.

You watch it. You watch for a whole night. And you find that you cannot stuff the word "evil" into its body. The word is too big. Too big. A wolf cannot contain it.

## III. Now Look Back at Humans

Now look back at humans.

A baby, not yet born. In his mother's phone there is already a group chat: "Expectant Mothers' Exchange." The group is discussing: boy or girl? What name for a boy? What name for a girl? A boy must be raised poor. A girl must be raised rich. A boy must be brave. A girl must be gentle.

He has not been born yet. The label "brave" is already waiting for him. Or the label "clever." Or the label "gentle." Or "good woman," "bad man," "a useful person," "a useless person."

He is labeled first, and born second. He is priced first, and exists second. He is tagged first, and only then "becomes himself."

Look at the bee. It has no "purity." It does not need "chastity" to confirm its "innocence." It simply is.
Look at the wolf. It has no "evil." It does not need "evil" to judge itself. It simply is.
Look at the human. Humans have "chastity." Humans have "strength." Humans have "independence." Humans have "innocence." Humans have "unworthy of existing."

The bee gathers nectar. The wolf hunts. The human is being labeled.

## IV. You See the First Sentence

You watch the bee. You watch the wolf. You watch them be. You see they do not live inside "industrious." Not inside "evil." Not inside the frame of human language.

But they have a world. The bee has the flowerbeds. The wind. The sunlight. The hive. The larvae. The wolf has the grassland. The moon. The howl. The territory. The mate.

Their world is not inside the frame of language. But they have a world.

And so —

The boundary of language is not the boundary of the world.

## V. You See the Second Sentence

You keep watching the bee. You watch it gather nectar. Does its gathering have "meaning"?

Yes. Its "meaning" is the gathering itself. Not "industrious." Not "selfless devotion." Just the gathering. It drinks nectar because the flowers are there. It flies back to the hive because the larvae are there. Its "meaning" is in the flowerbeds. In the wind. In the sunlight. In the mouths of the young.

You keep watching the wolf. You watch it hunt. Does its hunting have "meaning"?

Yes. Its "meaning" is the hunting itself. Not "evil." Not "law of the jungle." Just the hunting. It ambushes because the prey is there. It pounces because hunger is there. Its "meaning" is in the grassland. In the wind. In the howl. In the breathing of its young.

Their "meaning" is not inside the frame of language. But they have "meaning."

And so —

The meaning of the world must lie beyond language.

## VI. Now Look Back at Yourself

Those words — you cannot contain them either.

They are too big. You cannot hold a single one of them.

The bee cannot contain "industrious." The wolf cannot contain "evil." You cannot contain any word at all.

You do not need any word to confirm that you are.

You simply are.
