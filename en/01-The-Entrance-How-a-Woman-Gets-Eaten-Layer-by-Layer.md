# How a Woman Gets Eaten, Layer by Layer

Moral judgment is the largest massacre in human history.

It is the oldest, most stubborn, and most precise instrument of torture human civilization has ever built. It does not shed a drop of blood, yet it can nail a living person to the defendant's bench and eat her to the bone.

The power system of this instrument is a currency.

## 1. The Only Hard Currency: The Chastity Coin

For thousands of years, human civilization has issued exactly one hard currency. It is called the Chastity Coin.

Issuer: the Patriarchal Narrative Bank. Collateral: women's bodies. Credit guarantee: slut-shaming. Time of issue: several thousand years, uninterrupted.

Today the Chastity Coin circulates as four banks and four derivative currencies. One essence: mortgage the female body, issue counterfeit. Face value: ten billion. Zimbabwe dollars.

## 2. The Four Banks

1. The Traditional Bank issues the Duty Coin. Loan terms: keep your place — marry an honest man, serve your husband and raise your children, faithful to the end.
2. The Cinderella Bank issues the Prince-Charming Coin. Loan terms: wait for the prince — stay kind and pure, and you will be chosen, and granted true love.
3. The Independence Bank issues the Busy-Bee Coin. Loan terms: rely on yourself — work hard, earn your own money, and a good life will follow.
4. The Big-Results Bank issues the Big-Results Coin. Loan terms: cash out — secure a big result while you're young, and you become the master of your own life.

Four banks, one headquarters. Four currencies, one collateral.

## 3. How the Machine Actually Runs

Step one: choose a bank, sign the loan.

You pick one and sign. You mortgage your body, your self, your desire, your dignity. In exchange you receive a derivative of the Chastity Coin, face value ten billion.

Step two: the account managers of the other three banks call you simultaneously to put you on trial.

Before the coin is even warm in your hand, the account managers of the other three banks arrive. They are not outsiders. They are your parents, your friends, your colleagues, your neighbors, women online you have never met. They call at the same time, text at the same time, say it at the dinner table, say it in the comment section.

All of them fly the same flag: it's for your own good.

The Traditional Bank's account manager says: "You chose Cinderella? Can fairy tales feed you? You think what you hold is the Prince-Charming Coin? It's counterfeit. Turn back now — marry an honest man."

The Cinderella Bank's account manager says: "You chose Tradition? You'll be his breeding machine for life. You think what you hold is the Duty Coin? It's counterfeit. Wake up — you deserve to be chosen by a prince."

The Independence Bank's account manager says: "You chose Big Results? You're selling yourself as merchandise. You think what you hold is the Big-Results Coin? It's counterfeit. With a man, you'll never have security. You must rely on yourself."

The Big-Results Bank's account manager says: "You chose Independence? How much does a month of work even pay you? You think what you hold is the Busy-Bee Coin? It's counterfeit. Cash out while you're young — that's what smart women do."

Step three: in the name of "for your own good," they prove, simultaneously, that you've been cheated.

They are not helping you. They have come to collect for their respective banks. In the gentlest voices they say the cruelest things. They make you feel that the road you chose is a dead end. That what you hold is counterfeit. That you were cheated. That you were, from beginning to end, a fool.

They are not afraid you'll choose wrong. They are afraid you won't choose. The moment you choose one, the other three can pounce at once and tear you to pieces in the name of "for your own good." The coin in your hand is shredded.

Step four: you sell the counterfeit at a bone-broken price.

They convinced you. You panic. You begin to doubt your own coin. You begin to sell. You dump the ten-billion Chastity Coin at a fraction of a fraction. And what do you get back? One sentence: "You should have listened to me." One sentence: "Now you know you were wrong." A lower credit rating.

Now, see the last piece of the machine clearly.

This is where women suffer more than men. A man faces a single narrative: "You should have money." A woman faces four narratives, and the four are mutually exclusive. Whichever she chooses, the other three besiege her. The harder she tries, the more torn she is. The smarter she is, the more it hurts.

And what is the essence of these four narratives?

All counterfeit. All "you should." All the syntax of power. All demanding that you sell a part of your body or your soul to satisfy someone else's sense of existence.

The traditional narrative wants you to sell your self to feed patriarchal authority. The Cinderella narrative wants you to sell your body to feed the male gaze. The independence narrative wants you to sell your time and energy to feed the logic of capital. The big-results narrative wants you to sell your dignity to feed the sexual monopoly of top-tier men.

Not one of the four narratives says, "You were already whole."

All of them say, "You should become something." All of them say, "You are not it yet — go redeem yourself." All of them are beating you into a beggar who must keep begging for the sense of existence.

## 4. Bankruptcy Goes to Court

Your hands are empty; the coin is gone. Now the trial officially begins.

The court has no fixed judge. Everyone is the judge. The judge is your parents, your friends, your colleagues, your neighbors, strangers online, your ex-boyfriend, your husband, your child. The court sits at every dinner table, in every group chat, in every comment section, in every corridor whisper.

On the plaintiffs' bench sit four banks, suing you at once. The Traditional Bank charges you with "failing womanly virtue." The Cinderella Bank charges you with "impurity." The Independence Bank charges you with "not being independent." The Big-Results Bank charges you with "having nothing to show." Four plaintiffs, four indictments, filed simultaneously.

The judge asks: "Defendant, do you plead guilty?"

You say: "Not guilty."

The judge says: "Then defend yourself."

You begin your defense. You say: "I chose a road. I did my best."

The judge says: "Your best? You chose tradition — why didn't you endure it to the end? You waited for the prince — why didn't he come? You chose independence — why didn't you succeed? You chose to cash out — where is your result?"

You say: "I was hurt."

The judge says: "Hurt? Who isn't hurt? You were hurt because you weren't strong enough."

You say: "I was deceived."

The judge says: "Deceived? You were stupid. You were greedy. You didn't love yourself."

You say: "I had my reasons."

The judge says: "Reasons? Who doesn't have reasons? Your reasons are just excuses for not being good enough."

You say: "I have my worth."

The judge says: "Your worth? Your body has depreciated. Your youth is gone. Your emotions are overdrawn. What worth do you have left?"

The more you defend yourself, the more it sounds like quibbling. The more you explain, the more it sounds like a confession. The more you prove yourself, the more you prove you are not worth it. Every reason you give is dismantled into evidence that "you are not good enough." Every circumstance you raise is translated into a footnote to "you deserved it." You spend your whole life defending yourself. And you discover that every word of your defense has been convicting you.

The final verdict:

The judge strikes the gavel. The sound comes from all directions at once and converges into a single judgment:

"Defendant, you are low. You do not deserve to be loved. Your body is dirty. Your existence is a burden. Produce more value — or you do not deserve to exist."

After the verdict comes the execution.

Execution is not beheading. Execution is keeping you alive. Alive, on the defendant's bench every day. Alive, on trial every moment. Alive, convicting yourself every time you look in a mirror. Alive, confirming "I am unworthy" with every breath. Alive, reminded "you don't deserve it" every time you want to be loved.

You must produce more value. You produce your youth, your body, your feelings, your dignity, your last scrap of self. The more you produce, the more it proves you were never enough. The more you produce, the more it proves you were always a beggar. The more you produce, the more it proves you never deserved to exist at all.

You produce everything. You still don't deserve it. Because the standard of "deserving" is not in your hands. It is in the judge's hands. And the judge will forever tell you: "Not enough."

## 5. Layer by Layer, Until She Is Eaten

**Layer one: appearance-shaming.**

"You're not that pretty, let's be honest." "Ten more pounds off and you'd be fine." "Your legs are too thick." "Your skin is bad."

She starts looking in mirrors. She starts dieting. She starts wearing makeup. She starts on cosmetic procedures. She spends her savings on surgery. She thinks: once I'm beautiful, they'll stop.

Result: she becomes beautiful, and new shaming arrives. "That's surgery, right?" "That's makeup, right?" "Would anyone look at you bare-faced?" "What happens when you're old?"

What was eaten: her time, her money, her health, her confidence.

**Layer two: age-shaming.**

"How old are you and still not married?" "Wait any longer and nobody will want you." "Thirty and still picky?" "Wait any longer and you won't be able to have children."

She starts to panic. She starts going on blind dates. She starts lowering her standards. She marries someone, anyone. She thinks: once I'm married, they'll stop.

Result: married, and new shaming arrives. "What's with your husband?" "You married badly." "Your taste is terrible."

What was eaten: her right to choose, her standards, her marriage.

**Layer three: personality-shaming.**

"You're too strong-willed — no man dares want you." "You're too soft — you had it coming." "You're too sensitive — it was just a joke." "You're not gentle enough." "You're not considerate enough."

She starts to change. She becomes docile. She becomes agreeable. She stops pushing back. She thinks: once I've changed, they'll stop.

Result: changed, and new shaming arrives. "You have no opinions." "You've got no personality." "You're like a block of wood."

What was eaten: her character, her edges, her self.

**Layer four: competence-shaming.**

"A woman — why work so hard?" "What's the use of earning so much — still nobody wants you." "However good your career, without a family you're a failure." "You're a mother and still working — neglecting your family." "You're a mother and not working — earning nothing."

She burns at both ends. She works, she raises the child, she cooks, she waits on her husband. She thinks: once I've done it all well, they'll stop.

Result: everything done well, and new shaming arrives. "Not well enough." "Your child wasn't raised right." "Your husband strayed — because you weren't good enough."

What was eaten: her energy, her health, her career.

**Layer five: hostage motherhood.**

"No children — what kind of woman are you?" "You can't even watch a kid — what use are you?" "If you really loved your child, you would..." "You're a mother — how could you..."

She is taken hostage by the word "mother." No child: selfish. A child: her own fault. Full-time mother: a dependent. Working mother: neglects the family. Succeeds: sacrificed her family. Fails: unfit to be a mother.

What was eaten: the right to her own womb, her time, her qualification to be a person before being a mother.

**Layer six: moral shaming.**

"Dressed like that — isn't that asking to be looked at?" "Out that late — isn't that consent by default?" "Why didn't you fight back? Not fighting back means you half wanted it." "You accepted his gifts — isn't that permission?" "You've been in relationships — what innocence do you have left?" "You're divorced — you've depreciated." "Good girls don't dress like that." "Decent girls don't wander around at midnight."

The logic of this layer: first place her inside a frame of guilt, then make her prove her innocence. She starts explaining. She starts proving. She starts apologizing. She thinks: once it's all explained, they'll stop.

Result: the more she explains, the more it sounds like quibbling. The more she proves, the more it sounds like confession. The more she apologizes, the guiltier she looks. And the standard of the "good girl" never stops moving. The only constant: it is forever defined by others, and she forever fails it.

What was eaten: her dignity, her innocence, her history.

**Layer seven: the perfect victim.**

"If you really didn't want it, why didn't you call the police?" "Why didn't you scream?" "Why did you stay in touch with him?" "Why didn't you leave immediately?" "You've had boyfriends before — prove this time wasn't consensual."

The logic of this layer: impose the standard of the "perfect victim" on her. She must have perfect resistance, a perfect police report, perfect trauma responses, perfect severance. One flaw in any link, and her pain is downgraded to "she half wanted it"; her testimony is ruled "there's more to this story."

It never asks, "What did he do?" It only asks, "Why didn't you perform according to the script?"

What was eaten: her pain, her testimony, her qualification to be a victim.

**Layer eight: identity-shaming.**

"You keep your place — you're a man's accessory." "You went independent — an unwanted old maid." "You cashed out — a shameless gold-digger." "You believed in Cinderella — a naive little fool."

She chose one road; the account managers of the other three call her daily. She starts doubting herself. She starts selling off. She starts negating herself.

Result: whichever road she chooses, she is condemned. However she lives, she is cursed. Whatever she does is wrong.

What was eaten: her choices, her direction, her sense of existence.

**Layer nine: slut-shaming.**

She has no flaw. She is beautiful, young, kind, capable, morally clean, clearly situated. She is perfect. There is no point of attack to be found on her.

"She must be faking it." "She must have done something behind the scenes." "She must have slept her way up." "She must be kept by someone." "She must be dirty underneath." "Someone groped her — what's the big deal, she lost nothing." "She's the immodest one — and she blames others?"

Why? Because if she is perfect — if she truly stood up on her own — then the credit system of all four banks collapses. Her existence is a negation of the entire currency system. So she must be defined as a slut. She must be defined as dirty. She must be defined as low. Otherwise other women will think: if she can, I can. And then the system collapses.

She cannot refute it. She cannot prove she is not a slut. Because "slut" is not a factual judgment — it is an existential judgment. Not "what did you do," but "what are you." She cannot prove what she is not. The more she proves, the more it looks like concealment. The more she defends, the more it looks like confession.

Once the label sticks, her pain is "self-inflicted," her resistance is "performance," her existence is "pollution." Countless injured women would rather swallow their pain than speak — because the price of speaking is to be stamped, by all of society, as "the slut."

She is destroyed. Not for anything she did. For the fact that she exists.

## 6. The Truth

Every layer of shaming is a filtering mechanism.

· Layer one eats the ones who aren't pretty enough.
· Layer two eats the ones who aren't young enough.
· Layer three eats the ones who aren't tame enough.
· Layer four eats the ones who aren't omnicompetent enough.
· Layer five eats the ones who won't give birth — and the ones who did.
· Layer six eats the ones who aren't clean enough.
· Layer seven eats the ones who didn't perform by the script.
· Layer eight eats the ones who weren't firm enough.
· Layer nine eats the ones too perfect for the first eight layers to eat.

Every layer tells you: you are not good enough. Every layer tells you: change. Every layer tells you: once you've changed, they'll stop.

But you change, and the next layer arrives. You can never finish changing. You are never good enough. You are forever in debt. You are forever proving. You are forever on the defendant's bench. You are forever awaiting the verdict. You are forever being eaten. Layer by layer, until at last slut-shaming eats you to nothing.

The ultimate purpose of this entire syntax is one thing: to leave you speechless. To make you judge yourself. To make you sit on the defendant's bench and argue the prosecution's case, convict on the judge's behalf. To make you die inside your own heart.

And when you are eaten, you don't even know who is eating you. Because the ones eating you are your parents, your friends, your colleagues, your neighbors, strangers online, your husband, your child. The one eating you is everyone. The one eating you is also yourself.

Why does this machine never lack executioners? Because execution is paid work.

Every judgment pays the judge a wage, settled same day: superiority. Judge you once, and they confirm once more that they stand on the right side; convict you once, and one more deposit lands in their account.

Shaming is not the evil of a few bad people. Shaming is a business. Bad people cannot be killed off. Businesses can be audited. The moment shaming is understood as a business, moral outrage gives way to audit.

This is the moral grammar of this world. What I critique is not any single perpetrator, not any class of judges. What I am liquidating is this grammar itself. It does not kill — it makes people kill themselves. It does not shed blood — it makes hearts bleed.

A woman's body is neither base nor chaste. It is whole. Women exist — they always did. There is no logic that stands inspection which can prove a woman must hand over a part of herself to patriarchy in exchange for the sense of existence.
